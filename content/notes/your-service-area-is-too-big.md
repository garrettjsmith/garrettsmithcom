Hope this finds you well.

When you [set up your Google Business Profile](http://gmbgorilla.com/google-my-business-setup-service), you probably made the same mistake everyone makes.

You set your service area to cover everywhere you'd theoretically be willing to drive.

50-mile radius. Three counties. "We serve the entire metro area."

Sounds smart. Cast a wide net. More potential customers.

Here's the problem: Google doesn't reward reach. It rewards proximity.

**How Google thinks about service areas:**

When someone searches "electrician near me" in Buckhead, Google is trying to find the most relevant electrician for that specific searcher nearby.

Your SAB in Marietta claims to serve all of Atlanta. So does every other electrician within 30 miles.

But there's one electrician with an address in Buckhead. They win.

Not because they're better. Because they're closer and more specific.

**The dilution effect:**

Wide service areas dilute your relevance everywhere.

You serve 50 areas from your home? You're not particularly relevant in any of them.

Your competitor serves 1 city, has a showroom, and landing pages too? They look like the local expert.

Google is trying to match searches to the most relevant business for geography AND service. "We go anywhere" isn't relevance. It's the opposite.

**What top performers do:**

They pick their core geography and go deep.

→ Service areas are hyper-local  
→ Dedicated landing pages for each service  
→ Content that references specific neighborhoods  
→ Reviews that mention those areas by name

They're saying "we're the expert here" instead of "we'll drive there if you pay us."

**The fear that holds people back:**

"But I'll miss leads from outside my service area."

Maybe. But you're probably already missing leads from inside your service area because you're not relevant enough to rank top 3 for relevant keywords there.

I'd rather rank #1 in 1 city than #7 in the 40 areas that surround it within 2 hours. The math works out better.

**How to think about it:**

1. Where do you actually get most of your business? Start there.
2. Where would you most like to grow? Add two or three.
3. Where are you just "willing to drive to"? Drop those.

Your service area isn't a coverage map. It's a relevance signal.

**I’ve done this before:**

A decade ago I started a service company locally. Eager to grow quickly:

→ We got a GBP  
→ Created pages for every city and town in the county  
→ Marketed ourselves via Ads far and wide

We captured over 3,900 leads in our first 3 years. A tremendous success on air to my Local SEO friends.

But the majority of leads we couldn’t actually serve. After 3 years of this, we pulled back to 3 towns.

The towns where we had the highest concentration of business.

Revenues continued to grow after, despite targeting fewer geographies. Your results may vary, but it’s something to think about.

The instinct most have with their GBP and SEO is to try and inflate their service area — not decrease it.

**The uncomfortable part:**

You might need to shrink to grow. Especially if you don’t have unlimited marketing budget.

Narrowing your service area feels like leaving money on the table. But if you're not ranking in the places you claim to serve, you're not getting that money anyway.

Tight and dominant beats wide and invisible.
