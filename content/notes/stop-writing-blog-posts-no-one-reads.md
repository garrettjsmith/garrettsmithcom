Here's where most locally focused businesses waste their content budget in 2026.

They hire a writer. The writer produces two blog posts a month. "10 Signs You Need a New Roof." "How to Choose the Right HVAC System."

2,000 words. Internal links. Nice stock photos.

Nobody reads them. Nobody shares them. They rank for nothing.

Meanwhile, their competitor has 40 landing pages — one for service in every neighborhood they serve — and they're showing up everywhere in the local pack.

**The blog post myth:**

Somewhere along the way, everyone decided that "content = blog posts."

It made sense for a while. Google rewarded fresh content. Long-form articles built authority. Blogging was the strategy because bloggers linked to other bloggers.

And links powered the index. But for local businesses, the math never really worked.

Here's why:

Your customers aren't searching "how to unclog a drain." They're searching "plumber in Riverside" or "emergency plumber near me."

Those searches don't need 2,000 words of education. They need proof you serve that area and you're legit, open now, and can unclog the drain.

**What actually ranks for local intent:**

Location pages. Service + area combinations.

- "Roof repair in Buckhead"
- "HVAC installation Alpharetta"
- "Emergency plumber Decatur GA"

Each page targets one service in one area. 600-1,000 words of genuinely useful, specific content. Not keyword stuffing; actual information about serving that community.

Google's own data shows local results are hyper-localized down to neighborhood level now. A page targeting "Atlanta" loses to a page targeting "Midtown Atlanta" for someone searching from Midtown.

**The math:**

One blog post per week = 52 posts/year, most ranking for nothing.

One location page per week = 52 pages/year, each targeting real searches with real intent.

Same effort. Wildly different results.

**What makes a location page work:**

- Service + location in the title and H1
- Specific mention of the area (neighborhoods, landmarks, context)
- Driving directions, nearby businesses, and landmarks
- Your actual service details for that area
- Images of service rendered, the location, and staff
- Clear contact form and or CTA with offer
- NAP consistent with your GBP and other map profiles

That's it. Not complicated. Just specific.

**The uncomfortable part:**

Your writer likes producing blog posts because they don’t know any better and are a writer, not an SEO. Long form blog posts are often easier to write than digging into unique aspects of a business in a geography. Generic advice found anywhere also doesn't require as much research or risk of error.

Location pages require knowing your actual service areas, understanding local context, and building 30-50 pages in a procedural fashion instead of 12 freeform creative essays.

It's more work upfront. But it's work that actually compounds.

And doesn’t require you to be on the content hamster wheel.
