Hey!

I had a recent thought I wanted to share with you.

I’ve been in SEO for 22 years.

First time I've caught myself thinking: "Maybe I should just buy a booth at the tradeshow."

Not because SEO stopped working. It works.

But the math HAS changed.

Let me explain.

**Old math:** SEO is cheaper than offline, more trackable, better ROI. That's why we all went digital in the first place.

**New math:** Good local SEO costs about the same as local TV now. And you've got the same headache proving what actually worked.

Here's what Google's own AI Overview says about search right now:

- 97% of queries have rich elements (AI summaries, local packs, carousels)
- Reddit and forums are outranking traditional articles
- AI answer boxes are eating clicks before anyone hits your site
- Google's showing content snippets, not ranking full pages anymore

For local it's even more specific:

- Results are hyper-localized down to zip code
- Service area pages are beating generic content
- "Near me" searches turning into visits 24 hours later
- Your rankings change street to street

_The Google is telling you exactly what it wants_.

Yet most are still fighting upstream or aren't paying close enough attention.

**Three things actually working right now (you might not like this)**

Look, having an optimized GBP is great.

But it’s not the revenue rocket it was 5-10 years ago.

The brands we work with that are winning the SERPs have gone beyond the pack.

**1. Optimizing for LSAs **

Ads? Ads?!? You’re talking about ADs!!!

I know, I know, you simply can’t ignore LSAs anymore.

"Set it and forget it" is over. Markets are oversubscribed. The winners are tracking who's ranking, who's moving, what shifted this week.

We're watching LSA markets like hawks because this isn’t the same game as 2-3 years ago.

The best performers in your market have already optimized the sales conversation for conversion - and so that Google’s AI knows what they do too…

Emerging AI voice agents here. They answer fast and deliver the optimized pitch every time.

**2. Hyper-local pages. Lots of them.**

Not blog posts. Nobody's reading your "Ultimate Guide to HVAC in Phoenix" greenfield pillar.

They have an AIO and ChatGPT digest it for them.

Neighborhood-level landing pages though? One service, one geo, one page. That's what Google still hasn’t fully displaced with its owned properties.

That's what AI models also need to recommend you to users.

We're building these at a pace that would've seemed insane two years ago. Because it works.

**3. Showing up where Google sources information**

This is the one everyone ignores.

Google and AI models are surfacing information from sources beyond the most popular pages on a topic.

Their training data uses sources like Reddit, local forums, niche directories, and socials media. It’s placed an increased premium on what users are saying about you where they live online.

Your GBP can be perfect.

But if you haven’t joined the /r/DesMoines subreddit, you're invisible to the systems pulling from it.

Plus missing 5-15 leads a month because a thread you’re mentioned in positively ranks #2 for a popular term.

**Bottom line:**

All of this costs a lot more money to execute because there are more moving parts.

This isn't Search Engine Optimization anymore. It's become Search Everywhere Optimization.

Optimizing your presence in every Google SERP section. Organic AND Paid.

Optimizing your presence on every source AIOs and LLMs pull from. Owned, Paid, AND User Generated.

The SERPs are more fractionalized and where customers are going to discover is becoming increasingly so as well.

Which means there’s a lot more work for marketers to do (even with a squad of OpenClaw’s).

So you've got two levers:

1. Increase your paid budget
2. Increase your “search everywhere” budget

Then again, you could also start buying TV ads.

(Those still work too.)
