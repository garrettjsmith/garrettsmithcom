Hola!

Quick test.

Go look at your Google Business Profile photos right now. I'll wait.

Do you recognize the place?

Here's what most people don't realize:

Google's Vision AI looks at your photos. Not just to show them to customers — to understand what your business actually does.

It's reading your images to categorize your services, verify you're real, and decide how relevant you are to specific searches.

One case study I found online showed a lawn care company using a stock photo of a skunk for pest control. Google's Vision AI associated the image with "animal removal" instead of "lawn care." Ranking dropped from #2 to #3. They replaced the stock photo with a real job photo. Ranking restored.

That's not a coincidence. That's the algorithm reading your images.

**What bad photos signal:**

- Logo as your main image = "This business didn't try"
- Stock photos = Google's duplicate detection flags these as "non-unique" and buries them
- Blurry shots from 2019 = "This business might be closed"
- No photos of actual work = "We can't verify what they do"

**What Google wants to see:**

- High-resolution images (not phone photos from 2017)
- Photos of your actual work, team, location
- Recent uploads (recency matters here too)
- Variety: interior, exterior, team, jobs completed, equipment

And this isn't soft advice. Google's own data says businesses with photos get 42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks than businesses without photos.

BrightLocal studied 45,000+ listings and found businesses with 100+ images get 520% more calls, 2,717% more direction requests, and 1,065% more website clicks than the average business.

Even Localo's analysis of 2 million profiles showed businesses ranking in positions 1-3 have an average of 250+ images. Positions 11-20? About 170.

More photos. Better photos. Fresh photos. It all compounds.

(Google will even pull other images they find for your business around the web if the one(s) you uploaded aren't "good enough" for them.)

**The visual trust stack:**

Think of it in layers. After managing GBP profiles for Fortune 500 clients for 13+ years, this is the framework I use whenever possible:

1. **Exterior shot** — Proves you have a real location (even for SABs, show the van)
2. **Front or main entrance** — Proves you can visit your location
3. **Interior/workspace** — Proves you're operational
4. **Team photos** — Proves there are humans
5. **Work examples** — Proves you do what you say
6. **Recent uploads** — Proves you're still active

Missing any of those? You're leaving ranking signals on the table.

(Double if you're using poor images in your LSA campaigns.)

**The video piece:**

Google's pushing video hard now. A 30-second walkthrough of your shop or a quick "here's what we do" clip goes further than 20 photos.

Most competitors aren't doing this at all. Easy edge. You have a cellphone with a camera.

**What actually fixes this:**

- Delete your logo as your cover image. Lead with your best work photo.
- Upload 5-10 new photos this month. Mix of categories.
- Add one video, even if it's rough. Done beats perfect.
- Set a reminder to add new photos monthly. Consistency > one big upload.

**The uncomfortable part:**

Birdeye's 2025 State of GBP report found that businesses with high-quality, recent photos consistently outperformed peers on engagement — clicks, calls, and direction requests. Verified profiles with just 15+ photos saw stronger engagement across the board.

You're probably losing to competitors with worse reviews because their photos look more legit and more recent.

Takes an afternoon to fix. Most people never bother. Even when asked every month.
