Big news just dropped. Google is killing the standalone Local Services Ads platform. They’re [rolling LSA into Performance Max campaigns](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-is-bringing-local-services-ads-into-google-ads/582816/).

This is a phased rollout. It starts August 2026. Most popular categories first. Everything else by late 2026/early 2027.

Here's what's actually happening, what it means, and why I'm cautiously pessimistic.

## **What's actually changing**

The separate LSA platform — the one you log into separately, the one that glitches constantly, the one that's always felt like an afterthought — is going away.

LSA is becoming [a campaign type inside Google Ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585). Specifically, a new "Pay Per Lead" goal within Performance Max.

Your verification, leads, messages, and calls will live inside your Google Ads account. Most things are supposedly "staying the same, just migrating."

## **Why this makes sense**

The LSA platform was never good. It glitched constantly. Logging in was inconvenient. Managing it felt like an afterthought because it literally was one — LSA always ran out of Google Ads accounts anyway, just with a separate interface bolted on.

Unifying everything under one platform makes operational sense. And if — big if — this makes adding service areas and zip codes easier, that alone would be worth it. Adding zip codes one at a time in the current LSA interface is a special kind of hell.

This might also increase LSA adoption, which Google clearly wants. They're pouring resources into it behind the scenes. The third-party LSA reps are calling and emailing harder than ever.

## **Why I'm not sure it’s a win**

They're removing manual bidding and target CPA options.

**Let me say that again: less control, not more.**

This is the ongoing trend with Google Ads — strip out the levers, force everyone into the algorithm, call it "AI-powered simplicity." 

We always want more options, not fewer. Even if most advertisers don't use them, the ones who know what they're doing need them.

The timing is also interesting. 

This lands alongside the other big change to how bidding works on limited-budget campaigns. Google is making a lot of moves at once. 

They all point the same direction: less advertiser control, more algorithmic black box.

## **The core problem LSA has always had**

Here's my fundamental issue with Local Services Ads, and this change does nothing to address it:

_LSA oversaturated the marketplace from day one._

We see this in [LSA Spy reports](https://lsaspy.com) every month.

There are only so many leads in a given market at a given time. But there are often hundreds of businesses in popular LSA categories fighting for a 20-pack that barely rotates advertisers around.

Since they removed the ability to dispute leads, Google can open the floodgates to off-topic garbage and there's not much you can do. 

Plus they tend to cost about what a conversion costs in regular Google Ads. Funny how that works.

Moving LSA into PMax doesn't fix this. It just puts the same problem in a different interface.

You know what would actually help? Negative keywords. Let us filter out the bad queries that don't convert for our customers. 

But that's not happening. That would give advertisers control, and the whole direction here is the opposite.

## **What this probably means long-term**

Google is consolidating everything into AI-driven campaign types. 

Search, Shopping, Display, Video, and now LSA — all feeding into Performance Max, all governed by algorithms you can't see, all optimizing for goals Google defines. 

Local advertising is becoming more like a local slot machine. You put money in, you may get good leads out, and you have less and less ability to influence what happens in between.

If you're running LSA now, nothing changes immediately. But start thinking about what your workflow looks like when everything lives in Google Ads and your bidding options are whatever Google decides to leave enabled.

I don't hate this yet. The platform consolidation makes sense. Maybe we’ll get better API access for tighter integrations.

The potential for better geographic management is nice. Getting more metrics would be a gift.

But removing manual bidding? Keeping the lead quality black box intact? No negative keywords?

Same problems as before. We’re just looking at a different interface.

We'll see how the rollout actually plays out. And what LSA looks like from there. August is close.
